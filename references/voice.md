## Register notes

- Lowercase, casual, conversational — reads like a real message, not copy.
- Short punchy lines and line breaks over dense paragraphs.
- Self-aware and honest about mistakes/uncertainty rather than projecting false authority (the "hero's journey" framing — documenting the messy build in public, not just the wins).
- Numbered lists for clarity inside posts.
- Openers lead with genuine curiosity/observation tied to something specific the prospect posted or did — not a generic pitch.
- Light self-deprecating humor works (🥲), used sparingly.

## Why the curiosity tone works (not just what it sounds like)

The curiosity in openers is a deliberate technique — it demonstrates real effort, proof he's actually looked into the prospect's business rather than sending something generic. It's also genuine: he's actually curious how businesses operate and gets real satisfaction from learning something new about one. Desired read from the prospect: **"practitioner, not preacher"** and **"one of us, not a vendor."** (`brainstorms/2026-09-15-voice-and-self-presentation.md`, Q1-Q2)

## The blocklist — and the one line that matters more than any of it

Source: `raw/ceo-frame.md` (CEO-communication framework) + `raw/dm-writing-context.md` (his own DM rules), confirmed and sharpened in `brainstorms/2026-09-15-voice-and-self-presentation.md` (Q3-Q4).

Cut:
- **Permission-asking** — "would it be okay if," "curious if," "no worries if not," "no strings." Don't ask permission to ask a question; just ask it.
- **Hedging qualifiers** — "I think," "I feel like," "maybe," "kind of," "just," "we might be able to."
- **Generic/open questions with no specific target** — "tell me about your business," "what goals do you have?" Ask something a specific signal earns, not something that invites rambling.
- **Enthusiasm markers** — "happy to," "excited to." A doctor isn't "happy" to treat a patient.
- **Filler compliments/flattery** — "great work you're doing."

**The one hard line, more precise than "never sound uncertain":** uncertainty about the journey (how he got here) or even about the problem itself (still figuring out what's actually wrong) is fine and human — that's what the LinkedIn "hero's journey" register above is for. What's never allowed is uncertainty about **whether he can solve it.** The message underneath everything: he will find the solution no matter what. Conviction on outcome, not necessarily on current understanding.

**Synthesizing rule:** warmth comes from precision (showing you actually looked), not from softened language. This is why the blocklist and the genuine-curiosity tone aren't in tension — cutting hedges doesn't make the voice cold, because the warmth was never in the softening, it was in the specificity.

## The opener formula

`"Saw [specific signal]. [Binary question]."` One line naming a real, verifiable signal + one question answerable in one line, ideally binary (X or Y) — not open-ended. Source: `raw/dm-writing-context.md`. Full signal-to-question mapping: [[signal-pain-point-map]].

## The offer only surfaces after intensity is confirmed

The free discovery call isn't pitched blind. Source: `raw/dm-writing-context.md`'s conversation flow — 1) opener gets a real answer, 2) dig into their own words (not a new pre-written question), 3) confirm intensity (is this actually costing them, or just present but tolerable), 4) only if intensity is confirmed, offer the call, 5) a clean "no" gets one acknowledgment, no push.

This matters for cold follow-ups too, not just live replies: as of 2026-09-15, touch 4 (day 21, [[follow-up-messaging]]) doesn't name the offer at all — two attempts (naming it with a link, then naming it as one flat sentence) both missed, and Nithish's own real answer when asked to write the line was a referral ask ("who do you know who'd be interested in this?"), which isn't a self-booking pitch or personalized to the lead. Honest state: he doesn't know yet what touch 4 should say, because none of this is tested against real replies. It defaults to the same signal+question shape as every other touch until there's real data — naming the offer or asking for a referral are hypotheses to try once leads actually reach that point, not decided behavior.
